First 90 days

Listen, standardize, then scale

Before changing anything, understand how the team sells today. Fix data and rules next, then automate and train.

Days 1 to 30

Audit and learn

  • Meet each regional sales lead, FAE and the Finance contact
  • Map the current sales process and CRM objects end to end
  • Run a baseline data quality scan per region and owner
  • Collect the existing reports and who uses which one
  • List the top 10 pain points, ranked by impact
Days 31 to 60

Standardize

  • Agree stage definitions and exit criteria with Sales leadership
  • Set required fields and validation rules per stage
  • Launch the weekly data quality report and correction loop
  • Align forecast categories and the monthly forecast calendar
  • Clean duplicates on the top 100 accounts
Days 61 to 90

Scale and train

  • Publish the EMEA Sales Ops handbook and SOPs
  • Run CRM training per region (short, recorded, with a quiz)
  • Release the management dashboard with agreed KPIs
  • First forecast accuracy review and process retro
  • Agree the improvement backlog for the next quarter
Principles

How I work

One source of truth

If it is not in the CRM, it did not happen. Reports come from the CRM, not from private spreadsheets.

Make the right way the easy way

Fewer fields, smart defaults, clear rules. Sales should spend time with customers, not with forms.

Measure, then improve

Every process change has a baseline and a target, and gets reviewed after one quarter.

Service mindset

Sales Ops exists to help sellers win. Follow up politely, explain the why, give credit to regions that improve.

Stage model

Stages that match the design-in cycle

Module revenue arrives months after the decision: the customer designs the module into their device, certifies it, then ramps production. Stages must show where a project is in that journey. Click a stage to see its rules.

CRM object model

Account, project, opportunity, shipment

One customer device often uses several modules (cellular, GNSS, Wi-Fi, antenna). Tracking a Project between Account and Opportunity keeps those linked and makes cross-selling visible.

Object 1Account

The customer company. Owner, region, segment, distributor, parent account. One record per legal entity, no duplicates.

Object 2Project

The customer's end device (for example a smart meter). Application, vertical, target launch date, lifetime volume.

Object 3Opportunity

One module family for one project. Stage, annual units, price, close date, next action, competitor.

Object 4Shipment / Revenue

Actual units and revenue from Finance or ERP, linked back to the project to measure forecast accuracy.

Value

How opportunity value is calculated

Annual value

Annual units × average selling price.

Used for pipeline coverage against the yearly target.

Lifetime value

Annual value × project life in years.

Used to prioritize design-in support and FAE time.

Weighted value

Value × stage probability.

Used as a sanity check against the rep's own forecast category.

Forecast method

Three categories, one calendar

Reps call the number, the stage model checks it. If a rep's Commit is far from the weighted pipeline, that is a conversation, not an error.

Commit

90% or higher confidence

Design-win confirmed, purchase order expected or received, volumes agreed. Rep is accountable for this number.

Best case

Possible this period

Design-in or pilot running, customer timeline realistic, one or two open risks named in the record.

Pipeline

Not this period

Early stage or timeline unclear. Tracked for coverage and future quarters, never added to the forecast.

What the data says (demo)

  • Embedded World creates the most pipeline and the best return. Keep it as the main EMEA event.
  • MWC is expensive per qualified lead. It is a brand and partner event more than a lead event, so measure it on meetings, not only leads.
  • IoT Tech Expo is cheap but low conversion. Test a smaller booth with pre-booked meetings only.
  • Follow-up speed matters: leads contacted within 5 days convert far better than leads contacted after 2 weeks.
Checklist

Before, during and after every event

Before

Set it up to be measured

  • Goals: meetings, qualified leads, pipeline
  • Target account list shared with regions
  • CRM campaign created, scan app linked to it
  • Pre-booked meetings logged as activities
During

Capture properly

  • Every scan gets a quick note: project, volume, timeline
  • Hot leads flagged on the same day
  • Daily lead count shared with the team
  • Competitor and market notes collected
After

Follow up and report

  • Leads imported and de-duplicated within 2 days
  • Routed and first contact within 5 days
  • 30-day status check on follow-up rate
  • 90-day report: funnel, pipeline, cost, lessons
